Pipeline Design Lab · Three interface concepts

Make invisible networks visible.

Three interactive takes on the same idea, built on one shared fictional network: a Harvard Baseball player who wants to break into private equity, and the alumni who could help.

Comparison

A · Grid FirstB · Discovery FirstC · Pipeline First
Strengths
  • Most distinctive; unmistakably not LinkedIn
  • Overlapping Pipelines and bridges are visible at a glance
  • Path highlighting makes "how do I reach them" literal
  • Fastest time to value: a ranked, explained answer immediately
  • Works with a tiny network (shared affiliations drive results)
  • Swipe deck turns cold data into confirmed relationships
  • Matches how people already think ("my team", "my club")
  • Natural home for karma, membership rules and help requests
  • Community-level growth loop for launching Pipeline by Pipeline
Weaknesses
  • Can overwhelm first-time users
  • Needs a dense network to feel alive
  • Harder to make work on mobile
  • Can drift toward a generic "people you may know" feed
  • The Grid becomes secondary, so the differentiator is less visible
  • Quality depends entirely on the scoring
  • Cross-Pipeline discovery takes an extra step ("zoom out")
  • Small Pipelines may feel empty
  • Closer to existing group products
What to test with users Can a first-time user find a path to an alum in under 60 seconds? Do they understand solid vs dashed lines without the legend? Do users trust the "why" behind a recommendation? Do they finish a 10-card swipe session, and does it feel private enough? Do members return to help others (karma) without a direct personal benefit? Do they discover the cross-Pipeline view on their own?
Best signal Time to first introduction request Intro request rate per recommendation shown Weekly help responses per Pipeline

Questions for the meeting

1
Should the Grid be the homepage?

Compare A against B and C. A is memorable; B and C bring the Grid in when it answers a question.

2
Lead with personalized recommendations?

B. Try the "New user" toggle to see recommendations with only two confirmed connections.

3
Navigate primarily through Pipelines?

C. Harvard Baseball becomes a place, with its own Grid, activity and karma.

4
How does Surf the Grid stay intuitive?

All three use plain-language search with scope chips, explain each match, and only highlight what matters.

All people, firms and relationships are fictional. Harvard community names are used only to frame the proof of concept. No data leaves your browser.
